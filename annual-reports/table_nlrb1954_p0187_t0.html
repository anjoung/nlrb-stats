Reader’s notes

p. 187. Ten columns, two heading levels; "Temporary restraining order" spans the "Date issued"/"Date lifted" pair, and the other eight headings are tall single headings repeated in both levels. Footnote marker as cell content: in the "Date temporary injunction granted" column, eight rows (2-CC-230, 35-CC-22, 2-CC-246, 4-CC-43, 5-CC-29, 3-CC-26, 28, 29, and 4-CC-39) carry a raised "1" inside parentheses standing alone as the whole cell, i.e. a pointer to footnote 1 rather than a date. I transcribed these as "(1)". The footnote text itself is not on this crop — the table's bottom rule follows the last row with no footnote line beneath it, so footnote 1 must be printed elsewhere (the table appears to continue on the following page). `footnotes` is therefore empty. Ditto marks: "do" appears as the printed cell value (between leader dots) in the "Date petition for injunction filed" column for rows 5-CC-29, 5-CC-28, 30 and 1-CC-88; in the withdrawn/dismissed column for row 11-CC-3; and as "do." in the last column for row 4-CC-39. Leader dots dropped per instruction. "Type of petition": every row reads "10 (l)" (lowercase L, i.e. Sec. 10(l)) except row 2-CA-2554, 2907, where the letter is a "j" — consistent with that being the one CA (employer unfair-labor-practice) case in the list. The l/j distinction is small at this resolution but the glyph in that row clearly has a descender. Faded punctuation: this is a 1-bit scan and periods after abbreviations frequently drop out of the glyph while leaving their space. I restored them where the gap and the standard abbreviation make the reading unambiguous: "Assn." (rows 1, 3, 10), "Co." / "Inc." before a closing parenthesis (rows 2, 4, 12, 16), and the month abbreviations "Feb. 3, 1953" (row 11-CC-3), "Mar. 25, 1953" (24-CC-19), "Sept. 21, 1953" (24-CC-19) and "Dec. 17, 1953" (20-CD-33). "et al" in row 13-CC-5, 7 is left without a period, as printed. Two cells carry a parenthetical qualifier on a second printed line under the date, joined here into one cell: "Mar. 25, 1953 (consent)" (24-CC-19) and "Apr. 28, 1953 (consent)" (3-CC-27). Likewise the last column of 24-CC-14, 15, 16 reads "Jan. 16, 1953." with "(stip)." on the line below, joined as one cell. The "Date injunction denied" column is empty for all nineteen rows (dash rules or blanks only). Row 15 reads "AFL-Electrical Works, Locals 79, 181, 35, and 1261" — "Works" rather than "Workers" appears to be a misprint in the source (compare row 12, "AFL-Electrical Workers"); transcribed as printed. Stray ink: small specks sit in the "Date lifted" column of row 4-CC-37 and the "Date temporary injunction granted" column of row 11-CC-3, and a short mark crosses the dash rule in the "Date injunction denied" column of row 2-CC-230. These are scan artifacts over dash rules, not values; those cells are "".

The leftmost cell on this page is the section-of-the-Act column ("10 (1)"); the case-identifying row labels almost certainly sit on the facing page, outside this crop. Seven ruled columns are present and every value has been placed under the rule it is printed between; most cells are leader-dash rules ("------"), transcribed as "". Footnote references: several cells contain nothing but a small raised "(1)" standing in place of a date. These are transcribed as (^1) — the raised 1 is a footnote marker, and the parentheses are printed around it. No footnote texts are printed within this crop (the area below the table's bottom rule is cut off, showing only the clipped tops of a line of type), so footnotes is empty. Doubtful area: in the band between the "Dec. 9, 1953" row (row 19) and the final row, a faint, broken line of leader dashes runs across all seven columns just above the solid dash line that carries "Aug. 14, 1953". I read that faint line as show-through / offset from the facing page rather than a separate table row, partly because other isolated faint specks appear elsewhere on the page (e.g., above the "July 14, 1953" row in columns 2-3, in column 5 opposite "June 15, 1954.", and in column 7 near the top and bottom), and partly because an entirely blank case row would be meaningless here. Only one "10 (1)" stub is legible in that band. If the faint line is in fact a printed row, then one all-blank row is missing from this transcription at position 20. Other individual cells worth flagging: row 8 column 6 is printed "Nov 17, 1953" and row 13 column 6 "Jan 22, 1954", both without the abbreviating period, and row 6 column 7 is "Aug 26, 1953." likewise — transcribed as printed. Row 14 column 7 reads "Sept. 17, 1954."; the year is slightly indistinct and could conceivably be 1953, which would fit better beside the "Oct. 26, 1953" in column 6. Typesetting that left no space after a period or comma ("Aug.10, 1953", "June15, 1954.") has been normalised to single spaces; no other wording was altered.
