p. 197. Ten columns, headed by a two-level header: "Temporary restraining order" spans the two columns "Date issued" and "Date lifted"; the other eight headings are tall single headings repeated in both levels. Crop/continuation: the table is cut off at the bottom — the last printed line is the 17-CC-29 row, and no footnote text appears on this image even though markers 1, 2 and 3 are used in the body. The table and its footnotes continue on the following page. Braced entry for case 16-CC-22: this one case number is printed with a tall brace grouping two parallel sub-entries, a Texas petition and an Arkansas petition. I have split it into two grid rows so each printed value stays in its own column and line: the first row carries the case number, the union/company (both printed once, spanning the brace) and the (Tex) line; the second row has an empty stub and the (Ark) line. The cells "May 4, 1954" (date temporary injunction granted) and the dash rules in the two restraining-order columns and the injunction-denied column are printed on the line that aligns with "Mar 19, 1954", so I placed them on the second row, but the surrounding brace suggests they may be intended to apply to the whole braced group. Footnote-reference-only cells: "Date temporary injunction granted" is a lone parenthesised raised 2 for cases 8-CC-29, 3-CC-42, 13-CD-36/37 and 2-CC-324; transcribed as "(2)" since the parentheses are printed and there is no accompanying value. Raised markers attached to values: "Feb 7, 1955^1" (20-CA-958) and "Sept 14, 1954^3" (13-CC-90). Type of petition column: all rows read "10 (l)" (section 10(l)) except 20-CA-958, a CA case, which reads "10 (j)". The letters l and j are small and similar in this type; I am confident of the 10 (j) because a handwritten arrow on the scan points at that cell, apparently flagging the same distinction. Periods after abbreviations are inconsistently rendered in this 1-bit scan: some survive ("Nov. 24, 1954", "Apr. 11, 1955", "Apr. 12, 1955", "Jan. 19, 1956", "Oct 24, 1955.", "Settled.") while others appear only as a gap before a closing parenthesis ("Bechtel Corp )", "Swift & Co )", "Pilot Freight Carriers, Inc )", "Texas Industries, Inc , and"). Where no period is visible I transcribed none and closed the stray space; the originals very likely printed "Corp.)", "Co.)", "Inc.)" etc. Likewise "Settled" for 13-CC-90 shows no final period while "Settled." for 3-CC-42 does. Dash rules ("------") filling a cell are transcribed as "". For case 14-CC-56 the two restraining-order cells appear genuinely blank rather than dash-ruled (a handwritten arrow stroke crosses that area), but either way they transcribe as "". Possible misprint, transcribed as printed: the union for 13-CC-90 reads "AFL-Retail Clerks and Local 1460 (Jewel Food Stores)"; the word "and" where a local-union designation would be expected looks like a typesetting error for something like "AFL-Retail Clerks Local 1460". Stub labels spanning two printed lines ("3-CC-40, 3-CD-9", "6-CD-33, 35", "16-CC-34, 43", "13-CD-36, 37.", "35-CC-28, 29.") are joined into one cell, and leader dots after the case numbers are dropped.
