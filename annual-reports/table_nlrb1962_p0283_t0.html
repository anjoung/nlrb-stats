p. 283. Crop/completeness: the table is cut off at the bottom of this image. It ends in the middle of the "After hearing opened, before issuance of intermediate report" block (last printed row "Dismissed"); later stages of disposition are not on this crop (the stage percents shown so far total only 90.6 of 100.0 in the All C panel). No footnote texts appear under the table on this crop, so `footnotes` is empty even though markers 1, 2, 3, 4 and 5 are used in the grid. Decimal points: this scan has dropped or faded most decimal points in the "Percent of cases closed" columns (they print as e.g. "100 0", "83 7"). I restored them to the document's one-decimal format. Each restoration is corroborated by arithmetic within the column: in every one of the seven panels the stage subtotal equals the sum of its rounded component percents (e.g. All C 83.7 = 15.1+39.5+29.1; All C 4.9 = 3.4+(5)+.6+.6+.2+.1; CC 7.9 = 5.0+1.6+1.3; CP 3.1 = 1.2+.7+.7+.5). The number columns likewise reconcile across panels (e.g. 1,288+280+280+62+8+90 = 2,008). "(5)" cells: in the All C and CA "Compliance with stipulated decision" row, the All C and CA "Dismissed" row (after hearing opened), and the CB "Withdrawn" row (after hearing opened), the percent cell holds a raised, parenthesized footnote reference 5 standing alone, with no numeric value. I kept the printed parentheses and digit as "(5)"; it is a footnote marker (conventionally "less than 0.05 percent"), not a value. CD column: small raised 2, 3 and 4 are printed immediately BEFORE the figures 62, 171 and 66 in the "Adjusted", "Withdrawn" and "Dismissed" rows of the "Before issuance of complaint" block; I moved them behind a caret after each value. CP / "Withdrawn" in the "After hearing opened" block: the "Percent of cases closed" cell carries a short dashed rule instead of a figure, so it is transcribed as "". The CP number cell on that line is a printed 0. Consistency check holds: CP subtotal .6 = .3 (Adjusted) + .3 (Dismissed). Stray marks: the CB "Number of cases" column has brace/bracket strokes in the scan — a "}" spanning 280 and 1,057, and bracket strokes beside 9/4 and beside 1/0. These are printing or microfilm artifacts, not characters of the values; they are not transcribed. Doubtful cells: (a) All C, "Compliance with stipulated decision" (after hearing opened), percent — reads ".2" though 15/13,319 recomputes to .1; I kept the printed .2, which is what makes the printed column subtotal 2.0 add up. (b) All C, "Compliance with stipulated decision" (after issuance of complaint), percent (5) — 7/13,319 is .053, marginally above the "less than .05" threshold the marker implies. (c) CP, "Dismissed" (after issuance of complaint), percent ".5" — the decimal point is visible here but the glyph is faint. (d) CA, "Dismissed" (after issuance of complaint), percent ".1" — 14/8,910 recomputes to .2; transcribed as printed. These are printed as-is, not corrected.

p. 284. CONTINUATION PAGE: no column headings are printed on this crop, so header_rows is empty. The grid has 15 columns: the stub plus 14 value columns, which are seven Number/Percent pairs (column rules group them 2-2-2-2-2-2-2). The earlier stages of the table and all headings are on the preceding page. PERCENT COLUMNS AND LOST DECIMAL POINTS: this is a degraded bitonal scan in which the leading decimal point of a percent value is dropped roughly half the time (e.g. the first row's total-percent prints as "8" with no visible point while the adjacent CA percent clearly shows ".9"; mid-value points likewise render as a gap, "1 1" for 1.1, "5 1" for 5.1). Because every percent column in this table carries one decimal place, I have transcribed all percent cells with the decimal point, including zero percents as ".0". If the original in fact sets an exact zero as plain "0" rather than ".0", every ".0" cell here should be read as "0"; the distinction could not be resolved at this scan quality. FOOTNOTE-5 CELLS: a parenthesized raised 5 stands in place of a value in many percent cells (meaning "less than one-tenth of 1 percent"). Since it is the whole cell rather than a marker attached to a number, it is written "(^5)".
