Reader’s notes

p. 36. 19 columns: a stub with no printed heading, then six fiscal-year groups of three columns each (Number / Percent of total / Number of workers involved). The stub heading cell is blank in the original. Three cells are printed on badly worn type where a 9 and a 0 are hard to tell apart; each was resolved by the table's own row/column arithmetic, and each resolved value makes every total in its panel balance exactly. A reviewer should re-check these three against a higher-resolution scan: (a) "Representation cases closed", 1938-39 Number = 2,339 (the glyph could be read 2,330; 2,339 is required by 6,569 - 4,230 and by all five percentages in that panel); (b) "Cases dismissed" in the unfair labor practice section, 1940-41 Number of workers involved = 296,597 (could be read 206,597; 296,597 is required for the column to sum to the printed 1,208,005, and 206,597 would make the all-cases dismissed figure exceed the sum of its two components, which is impossible); (c) "Cases settled" in the representation section, 1937-38 Number of workers involved = 339,898 (could be read 330,898; 339,898 is required for the column to sum to the printed 899,243 and for 594,121 + x = 934,019). Apparent inconsistency in the source, transcribed as printed: for 1940-41, "Cases closed by certification" shows 351,936 workers in the all-cases section but 383,576 workers in the representation section, although both sections report the same 518 cases. For every earlier year the two figures are identical. Both columns nonetheless sum exactly to their printed section totals, so neither value appears to be a misreading. The 1940-41 group heading is printed "Cases." with a trailing dot over the Number / Percent columns; transcribed as "Cases" since the dot is almost certainly a typographic artifact. Percentages under 1 percent are printed without a leading zero (".8", ".4") and are kept that way. Column rules, leader dots and the double rules that separate the three sections carry no data and are omitted.
