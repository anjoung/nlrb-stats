p. 168. Five data columns plus the stub. The stub heading "Year and month" and the tall heading "Number of workers involved in cases brought before the Board" each span the whole header block and are repeated in every heading level. The italic year lines (1935, 1936, 1937, 1938, 1939) are section labels with no values; each year's "Total" row is printed directly under its year label, above that year's months (verified: the 1937, 1938 and 1939 totals equal the sums of the months printed below them). May 1937, column (3): the cell holds only a small raised "(3)" footnote reference (footnote 3, "Figure not available"), not a number; encoded as "^3". The (1/3) cell of that same row and the (3) and (1/3) cells of the 1937 Total row hold dash rules, transcribed as "". October 1935, column (2): a raised 2 is printed before the figure, transcribed as "48,223^2". Two printed arithmetic discrepancies, transcribed as printed: (a) the 1936 Total in column (1), 523,138, exceeds the sum of the twelve 1936 monthly figures (523,128) by 10, although the 1936 totals in columns (2) and (3) match their month sums exactly; (b) the 1939 Total in column (2), 783,089, exceeds the sum of the twelve 1939 monthly figures (783,039) by 50, although the 1939 totals in columns (1) and (3) match exactly. I could not tell from the scan whether the error is in a total or in one month's digits; every printed ratio in those rows is consistent with the month figures as transcribed. The caption covers October 1935–June 1940, but the body printed on this page ends at December 1939 and is closed by a double rule with the footnotes below it; the January–June 1940 months are not on this crop.

The spanner "Number of workers involved in strikes" covers columns (2) and (3); "Ratio of cases to strikes" covers (1/2) and (1/3); "Number of workers involved in cases brought before the Board" is a tall single heading over column (1), so it is repeated in the first two heading levels, as is the stub heading "Year and month". Department of Labor, Bureau of Labor Statistics. The data in column 2 were taken ...". Because it is neither complete nor reliably legible, it is not transcribed in `footnotes`.
