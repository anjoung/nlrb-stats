p. 251. CROP IS INCOMPLETE. The table continues below the bottom of the image: the "Total" line gives 112 elections and 9,474 eligible voters, but the industry lines shown (Manufacturing 80 + Mining 3 + Wholesale trade 11 + Retail trade 7 = 101; eligible voters 8,152 + 150 + 385 + 378 = 9,065) fall short, so roughly 11 further elections' worth of industry rows are cut off. The Manufacturing block itself is complete (its 17 detail rows sum to 80). The footnote keyed to the "Industrial group ^1" stub heading is also below the crop, so `footnotes` is empty. DECIMAL POINTS. This scan drops or nearly erases the period in a large share of the one-decimal percent figures, so cells print as e.g. "10 7", "71 4", "25 0", "66 2". These are decimal points in the original (every percent column in this table is carried to one decimal, and each reconstructed value checks out against the counts and the 100.0 totals), so they are transcribed as "10.7", "71.4", "25.0", "66.2", etc., in the same spirit as the space-after-comma rule. No value was otherwise altered. ZERO CELLS. The table mixes a bare "0" and a leading-point ".0" in the percent columns, and at this resolution the leading point is the single least reliable mark on the page. Each zero cell is transcribed as the dot appears to me, but any individual "0" vs ".0" call in the four Winner percent columns should be treated as low-confidence. Three such cells I read as having a point that the surrounding rows would not predict: Fabricated metal products, C. I. O. Percent (".0"); Professional, scientific, and controlling instruments, C. I. O. Percent (".0"); Retail trade, C. I. O. Percent (".0"). PRINTED AS SHOWN, NOT CORRECTED. Two cells depart from what the counts would give and are left as printed: Primary metal industries, A. The "A. F. of L" heading is transcribed without a closing period after the L, as printed.
